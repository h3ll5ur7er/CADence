title: Joint frames on pattern copies of a hole point either way
type: bug
status: done
priority: medium
labels: [assemblies, joints, found-by-CAD-96]

The nose case has a tappet guide hole and a circular pattern of it (8 copies, 40° apart). Slider joints from each guide face (`f:guide:side:guide`, `…~2@bosses.1` … `…~2@bosses.8`) to the same face of nine identical tappets place tappets 1–4 and 9 pointing inwards and tappets 5–8 pointing outwards: the frames of copies 4–7 have their axis the other way round from the original's. Each needed `flip: true`, and a positive slider value moves those tappets inwards instead of outwards (the cam links need ratio −1). Found in [[CAD-96]]. A copy's frame should be the original's frame turned with the pattern.

Seen on the way:

- `edit-joint` with an empty name leaves the name empty (`add-joint` gives "Slider 5").
- Cam profiles are interpolated with Catmull-Rom, which dips below a flat base circle next to a lobe (a lift of −0.024 mm where every point is 0): a follower sinks into the cam. A shape-preserving interpolation (monotone cubic) would not.
- The interference check counts parts that only touch: a link rod's eye on its knuckle pin (both 10 mm) is reported with 0.07 mm³, wrist pins in link eyes with 0.001 mm³, barrels seated on the crankcase with 0.1 mm³. The guide says parts that only touch do not count.

- [x] Frames of cylindrical faces (and of copies made by patterns) follow the original's direction
- [x] `edit-joint` with an empty name gets the default name, like `add-joint`
- [x] Ignore overlaps below a tolerance (or of touching faces) in the interference check

**Done (phase 27).** A round face or edge of a pattern copy (its name ends in `@<pattern>.<n>`) points its axis the same way round as the original's. Existing joints that needed `flip: true` on copies 4–7 now point the other way: take the flips (and the cam links' ratio −1) out. `edit-joint` with an empty name keeps the joint's name, or gives the default. Cam profiles are monotone cubics. Overlaps below a ten-thousandth of the smaller part's volume are touching.
