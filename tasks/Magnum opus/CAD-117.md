id: CAD-117
title: Joint frames on round faces depend on the face's axis-aligned box
type: bug
status: done
priority: high
labels: [assemblies, joints, patterns, found-by-CAD-96]

A joint on a round face sits on the face's axis at the point nearest the centre of the face's axis-aligned bounding box ([[src/core/engines/modeling/occt-modeling-engine.ts#L472-L490]]). An axis-aligned box is not rotation invariant, and OCCT's box of a face depends on whether the face has a triangulation yet. So:

- **Pattern copies get different frames from the original.** The nine tappet guides of the radial engine's nose case are one hole and a circular pattern of it (40° apart, same shape). In a new process, the frame origins along the guide axis differ by up to 0.8 mm between guides: the slider constant (tappet origin minus slider value) came out 67.09, 67.47, 67.81, 67.32, 67.89, 67.20, 67.77, 67.12, 67.62 mm for guides 1 to 9. Nine identical cam links therefore put nine identical tappets at nine different heights.
- **The same assembly solves differently in different sessions.** The MCP server (whose parts were meshed for pictures) gave one constant for all nine guides, while a new `cadence run` process gave the values above. Part of that may be [[CAD-116]], but the box-based centre alone can move a joint between sessions.

Found in [[CAD-96]] round 3, setting the tappets' cam links from measured constants: the offsets were right in one process and 2.6 to 3.4 mm off in another.

- [x] A round face's (or circular edge's) frame origin is a rotation-invariant point: the middle of the face's extent along its axis (or its centroid projected on the axis), the same for every pattern copy and in every session
- [x] Test: joints on the original and on the copies of a circular pattern of holes give the same offset along each hole's axis

Fixed in phase 28 ([[Architecture/Decisions#ADR-47]]): the origin is the middle of the face's parameter range along its axis (`UVBounds`; on a cone, along the slant times the cosine of its half angle); a straight edge's is its midpoint. The nine copies of a radial hole through a ring now agree to 0.0001 mm (the kernel approximates each copy's intersection curves anew), meshed or not; before, 0.04 mm in the test and up to 0.8 mm on the engine. Joints on faces that end on curved faces may sit up to a millimetre differently than before: the tappets' slider constants have to be measured again.
