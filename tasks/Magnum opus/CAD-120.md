id: CAD-120
title: Setting a driving joint far from its value flips a closed loop into its mirror pose, and the flip is saved
type: bug
status: todo
priority: high
labels: [assemblies, joints, kinematics, found-by-CAD-96]
---

Checking the radial engine of [[CAD-96]] (round 3) for a collision the motion study found at 0.9 s, I set the crank straight to that moment with `set-joint-values` (`j_crank` 0 → 324°) in a `cadence run` script, then back to 0. Afterwards the engine was broken for every client: the master rod stood at −540°, each link rod about 180° off, and the pistons had slid through the crankcase to the other side of the axis (piston 1 at z −85 instead of +135; `check_assembly` in the MCP server: 140 overlaps, crankshaft and pistons up to 16,500 mm³).

The solver closes loops by Newton steps from the stored values ([[src/core/engines/kinematics/kinematic-solver.ts#L160-L200]]), and the solved values of every joint are saved with the document. A piston on a cylindrical joint may slide along the whole (infinite) cylinder axis, so the mirror pose (master rod turned half a turn, piston through the crankcase) satisfies every joint exactly. A jump of the driver lands in whichever pose is nearest to the old values, and nothing moves it back.

Reproduced on a copy (the round-3 backup imported into an empty folder, all parts loaded):

| step | piston 1 z | master rod `j_mr` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| start (crank 0) | 135 | 0 |
| crank 324 | 129.24 | 43.68 |
| crank 0 | 135 | 0 |
| crank 36, 72, 180 | 129.24, 115.13, 85 | −43.68, −84.48, −180 |
| **crank 0** (from 180) | **−85** | **−180** |

From 180° (bottom dead centre) back to 0 in one step, the master rod keeps −180° and the piston ends on the far side, with no error. With a slide limit on the nine piston joints (`min 0`) the mirror pose is excluded, but the same jump then fails: "The joints cannot all hold: this loop does not close with these values and limits." The motion study, which steps the crank in small increments, moves the engine correctly over many turns.

So one call with a big step (from a script or an agent; possibly also a value typed into the joint panel, not checked) silently puts an assembly into a pose its designer never made and saves it for everyone. Getting it back took restoring 27 joint values from the project history.

- [ ] Changing a driving joint's value moves there in steps (as the study does), so closed loops keep their pose; or at least the loop stays in the branch it was in
- [ ] When a loop changes branch anyway, the answer says so (joint values that jump by about half a turn)
- [ ] Test: the engine-like slider-crank set 0 → 180 → 0 ends where it started; with slide limits the jump solves
