The robot arm ([[CAD-182]], [[Robot arm]]) draws every link in its own joint frame and places the next joint with an offset on the link's origin. Two things made that hard:

- **No parameters.** The elbow sits `upper` (220 mm) along the upper arm, but the offset takes only numbers: change the project parameter and the arm grows while the joint stays at 220. A joint on a round face follows the geometry, but a joint between origins cannot follow a parameter.
- **Lean, tilt and angle.** Pointing a joint's axis along a link's +X takes lean 90° and tilt 90°, and then angle 90° to turn the next link the right way. Each of the 14 joints took working out by hand, and checking it with pictures and transforms. Agents think in directions: "axis along [1, 0, 0], the next link's x along [0, 0, 1]".

- [ ] Offset translate, lean, tilt and angle take expressions of the assembly's and project's parameters, and follow them
- [ ] An offset can give the joint axis as a direction (and the frame's x as a second one) in the instance's coordinates instead of lean and tilt
- [ ] get_assembly reports each joint's frame in world coordinates (origin, axis), so a wrong frame is seen without a picture
