Every pose of the robot arm's *Pick and place* study ([[CAD-182]]) was worked out by hand: a script computes the waist angle from the 52 mm side offset of the forearm, the shoulder and elbow from the two link lengths, the wrist pitch so the tool points down, and the flange roll so the jaw lines up with the cube. It took several rounds of setting joint values and reading transforms to find the signs and the zero positions. CADence has no way to say "put the gripper's fingertips here, pointing down" and get the joint values. A Stewart platform ([[CAD-183]]) needs the same for its plate. Its legs' lengths for a pose are easy by hand (the distance between each base ball and the plate's ball moved by the pose). But every tilt, turn and lift of its study was worked out by a script outside CADence and keyed as six leg lengths, and the plate's pose can only be checked from pictures ([[CAD-196]]).

- [x] A target for an instance's frame (a point and, optionally, directions) in an assembly, solved for the joints of the chain that leads to it, within their limits
- [x] For open chains of any length and for closed ones (the Stewart platform's plate)
- [x] When the target cannot be reached, the nearest pose and how far off it is; when there are several poses (elbow up or down), a way to choose one
- [x] Studies can key targets instead of joint values (the tool moves along a straight line between keys)
- [x] MCP: a command or tool that answers with the joint values, and sets them on request
- [x] Check: the robot arm's pick-and-place poses come out within 0.1 mm of the hand-made ones

Fixed in phase 45 ([[Architecture/Decisions#ADR-64]]): targets (an instance's point at a place, and optionally two of its directions) are residuals of the loop solver, so one solver reaches with open and closed chains. Only the joints leading to the targets' instances (and to loops) move; `hold` keeps joints; a target out of reach gives the nearest pose and the `miss`. Points are reached first, then directions; a missed target is tried again from four nearby starts, and steps are capped (0.2 rad, 20 mm), so an arm held straight bends the right way. The `reach` MCP tool answers with the values (`apply` sets them), the `reach` command sets them, and studies key `targets` that move along straight lines. Checked: from the upright arm, the four pick-and-place poses come out within 0.0004° of the hand-made ones with the fingertips 0 mm off. The Stewart platform's legs for a 10° roll come out within 0.0004 mm of the hand-made strokes.
