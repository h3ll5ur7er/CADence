The robot arm ([[CAD-182]]) has six steppers: NEMA 17 at the waist, shoulder and elbow, NEMA 11 at the forearm and wrist, NEMA 8 at the flange. Whether they can hold the arm is the first question of its design. CADence knows every part's mass and centre of mass (assembly_properties) and every joint's axis and pose, but it does not give the torque a joint needs to hold the arm against gravity in a pose. By hand: about 11 N·m at the shoulder with the arm out straight and 1 kg in the gripper. A NEMA 17 holds about 0.4 N·m, so the shoulder needs a gearbox of 30:1 or more.

- [ ] For a pose: each joint's static torque (revolute) or force (slider) from gravity (a direction and g) and the parts' masses, with optional point loads (a payload at an instance's frame)
- [ ] Over a motion study: the largest torque of each joint and when, optionally with the accelerations of the study's motion
- [ ] MCP: assembly_properties or a tool of its own answers with the torques
- [ ] Check: the robot arm's shoulder torque matches a hand calculation within 1 %
