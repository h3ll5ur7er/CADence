The robot arm ([[CAD-182]]) has six steppers: NEMA 17 at the waist, shoulder and elbow, NEMA 11 at the forearm and wrist, NEMA 8 at the flange. Whether they can hold the arm is the first question of its design. CADence knows every part's mass and centre of mass (assembly_properties) and every joint's axis and pose, but it does not give the torque a joint needs to hold the arm against gravity in a pose. By hand: about 11 N·m at the shoulder with the arm out straight and 1 kg in the gripper. A NEMA 17 holds about 0.4 N·m, so the shoulder needs a gearbox of 30:1 or more.

- [x] For a pose: each joint's static torque (revolute) or force (slider) from gravity (a direction and g) and the parts' masses, with optional point loads (a payload at an instance's frame)
- [x] Over a motion study: the largest torque of each joint and when, optionally with the accelerations of the study's motion
- [x] MCP: assembly_properties or a tool of its own answers with the torques
- [x] Check: the robot arm's shoulder torque matches a hand calculation within 1 %

Fixed in phase 45 ([[Architecture/Decisions#ADR-64]]): `joint_loads` gives each actuator's load by virtual work: each freedom moved a hundredth either way with the other actuators held, and the masses' work against gravity (and, with `dynamic`, their accelerations). It works as the assembly stands, or as the largest over a study with when. Actuators are the driven joints, or every joint of an open chain. Payloads are masses at points of instances. Checked on the robot arm, out straight with 1 kg at the fingertips: the shoulder holds 11.0991 N·m, the same to 0.0001 N·m as the moment sum of the parts beyond it, each motor in its own configuration.
