The robot arm's upper arm ([[CAD-182]]) was checked held at its ⌀12 shoulder axles, with 21.5 N on the elbow axle (everything after the elbow and a 1 kg payload). With the default element size, the answer looked safe and settled; finer meshes changed it by a factor of four:

| Element size | Elements | Largest stress | Deflection | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default (≈ 11.8 mm) | 1,350 | 2.75 MPa | 0.0385 mm | 2 s |
| 5 mm | 28,908 | 3.95 MPa | 0.0138 mm | 8 s |
| 3 mm | 124,464 | 8.36 MPa | 0.0195 mm | 37 s |
| 2.5 mm | 215,004 | 12.66 MPa | 0.0182 mm | 68 s |

By hand, the axles carry the 4.7 N·m about the shoulder in torsion: about 7 MPa shear in each, 12 MPa von Mises. The default size aims at about 800 cubes ([[src/core/engines/analysis/fea.ts#L252-L254]]), so the ⌀12 axles that hold the part are about one element across. The stress also keeps rising at the axles' sharp roots, where it has no finite value.

- [ ] The default size takes the smallest held and loaded faces into account (several elements across each), not only the part's box
- [ ] The answer says how settled it is: the result at the next coarser size beside it, or a warning when they differ by more than 10 %
- [ ] A peak at a sharp inside corner is reported as such (with the stress a little away from it), with a hint to round the corner
- [ ] Open point *mesh that refines where stresses peak* ([[Open points]]) is the larger version of this
