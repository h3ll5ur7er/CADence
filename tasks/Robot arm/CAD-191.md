The robot arm's upper arm ([[CAD-182]]) carries the forearm, wrist, gripper and payload, whose weight acts up to 340 mm beyond the elbow. The strength check takes forces spread over a face and pressures, so the load could only go on the elbow axle as 21.5 N. The moment the forearm's lever puts on the axle, about 4.4 N·m, is left out, and so is the arm's own weight (1.5 kg of aluminium, 220 mm long).

- [x] Gravity: the part's own weight from its material, along a direction
- [x] A remote load: a force at a point (and a moment), carried to a face as a rigid connection would (the forearm's weight at its centre of mass, onto the elbow axle)
- [x] A moment (torque) on a face about an axis (a motor's torque on a shaft)
- [x] MCP: `set-simulation` takes them; the user guide describes them ([[User guide/Measuring and checking]])

Fixed in phase 46 ([[Architecture/Decisions#ADR-65]]): `gravity` in the setup adds the bodies' weight from their materials. `remote` loads (a force and a moment at a point, carried to a face rigidly) and `moment` loads (N·m about an axis) join force and pressure. The panel has *Moment* and *Their own weight*, and shows remote loads. Tests match beam theory for the weight, a force 50 mm beyond the tip and a moment. On the upper arm, with its weight and the 21.5 N of everything after the elbow as a remote load at its centre (370, 0, 52): 17.9 MPa, against 9.0 with the force on the axle alone.
