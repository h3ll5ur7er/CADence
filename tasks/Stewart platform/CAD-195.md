The Stewart platform ([[CAD-183]], [[Stewart platform]]) has six legs between ball joints. Each leg is a slider, and its six lengths set the top plate's pose. `set-joint-values` moves one joint and lets every other free joint follow to close the loops. That suits one input (the radial engine's crank), but here all six legs are inputs:

| Asked (leg strokes, mm) | Got |
| --- | --- |
| leg 1 to 80, the others at 50 | 58.7, 53.0, 44.3, 40.9, 44.2, 50.0 |
| the six strokes of a 10° roll, in one `edit_assembly` | 63.5, 63.5, 50.5, 41.1, 32.9, 40.4 instead of 60.1, 63.1, 53.1, 47.6, 37.9, 40.4 |
| all six back to 50 | 11.1, 1.0, 18.7, 56.4, 59.9, 50.0: the plate rolled −20.7° and moved 41 mm |

Every command is solved on its own, so the legs set first are pushed away by the ones set after them. Only the last leg keeps its value. Writing all 18 joints' values with `edit-joint` (the ball angles too) ends 0.8 mm and up to 2 mm of stroke off. A motion study that keys all six legs holds them, which shows the solver can.

- [x] One command sets several joints' values together and solves once, holding all of them (`set-joint-values` with a list)
- [x] A joint can be marked as driven (an actuator): moving another joint never changes it, as keys in a study already do
- [x] When the held values cannot close the loops, the answer says so and by how much, and leaves the assembly as it was
- [x] Check: the six strokes of a 10° roll put the plate within 0.01 mm and 0.01° of the pose they were worked out for

Fixed in phase 44 ([[Architecture/Decisions#ADR-63]]): `set-joints` sets several joints' values in one change, solved once with all of them held, and refused (the assembly left as it was) when they cannot all hold, saying how far the loop stays open. A joint marked `driven` (an actuator, *Driven* in the joint's panel) is held whenever anything is solved; a driven joint that closes a loop holds too. Checked on the platform with this phase's server: the six strokes of a 10° roll, of a 20 mm shift with a 32.5 mm lift and a 15° turn, and of home each give exactly those strokes, and the plate lands at the pose they were worked out for (get_assembly rounds its transforms to three decimals, about 0.03°). Test: a planar platform on three legs in [[src/core/engines/kinematics/kinematics-engine.spec.ts]].
