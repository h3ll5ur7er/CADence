The Stewart platform's legs ([[CAD-183]]) sit between two ball joints, as a leg with universal joints at its ends would. A leg between two balls can also spin about its own axis, a freedom the real actuator does not have: the solver turns the base balls' roll by up to 2.6° while closing the loops. With round legs this cannot be seen; with a motor on the side of the cylinder it would be.

The legs' joints also have limits: a rod end tilts about 30° from its axis, in any direction. A ball joint's limits are per angle (yaw, pitch, roll about the frame's axes, applied in turn). That fits the base balls, whose pitch is the tilt from the vertical. It does not fit the top balls, whose angles mix the plate's pose and the leg's direction. A limit set below the home tilt (pitch ≤ 10° at one base ball, 13.2° at home) is kept without a word, and the other legs move the plate 13 mm to make up ([[CAD-186]]).

- [ ] A universal joint: two rotations about crossing axes (the frame's x, then the new y), no spin
- [ ] Ball joint limits as a cone: the largest angle between the two frames' z axes (and optionally the spin)
- [ ] Collision checks and studies report where a cone limit is reached
